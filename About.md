I am currently making the transition from physics to data science. I graduated cum laude with a BS in Physics from the National Institute of Physics, University of the Philippines Diliman, and recently completed my MS in Physics at the same institute. My research focused on gravitation, particularly the analytical and numerical modeling of astrophysical mass distributions around black holes. Alongside my physics work, I have been developing statistics and machine learning projects using publicly available datasets, both to deepen my understanding and to demonstrate what I can do with data.

The transition from physics to data science is not as abrupt as it might seem. The development of new instruments and increasingly sophisticated models has pushed astrophysics toward computational and data-driven research, whether to validate existing theories or expose their deficiencies. But this is, in a sense, what science has always done: observe the world, construct models, test them against evidence, and revise them when they fail. The same principle applies outside academia. We now have access to enormous amounts of data, and we can use them to test assumptions and evaluate decisions rather than simply accepting the status quo.

What draws me toward data science is therefore not simply an interest in learning another technical field. It is the opportunity to apply the way I have learned to think to problems outside physics. Intellectual curiosity has always pushed me to look beyond the boundaries of my field, and I want to put this way of thinking to work in places where the questions are different but the challenge is familiar.

The most valuable lessons I carry from physics are not how to construct the Lagrangian and Hamiltonian of this or that system, nor how to derive by hand the Christoffel symbols of a spacetime. They are learning how to find structure in a pile of seemingly unrelated facts, question an assumption, and keep working when the answer is not obvious. That is what I hope to bring with me as I move into data science. Physics has taught me how to approach difficult problems, but those lessons are only useful if I can apply them beyond the problems I already know. After all, the strength of the sword is not tested within the forge, but in the battles it won.
